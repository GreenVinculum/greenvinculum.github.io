Charging an EV is less mysterious than the discourse makes it sound. Once you sort **where** you charge most of the time, the rest becomes routine. This guide is the mental model I use for daily driving and road trips.

## The three speeds that matter

**Level 1 (120 V wall outlet).** Slow. Fine for a plug-in hybrid or a short commute if the car sits overnight every night. Painful as your only plan for a full-size EV.

**Level 2 (240 V).** The home / workplace workhorse. A proper wall unit or a NEMA 14-50 setup turns overnight into a full (or nearly full) battery for most cars. If you can charge at home, this is the upgrade that makes EV ownership feel easy.

**DC fast charging (DCFC).** Highway and “I’m in a hurry” charging. You pay for speed and convenience. Use it for trips and top-ups — not as your everyday default if you have a better option at home.

## Home charging: what actually helps

- **Hardwire or a dedicated circuit** when you can. Shared garage outlets and extension-cord hacks cause more problems than they solve.
- **Schedule charging** for off-peak rates if your utility offers them. The car does not care what time electrons arrive.
- **Leave headroom.** Parking at 100% every night is rarely required. Many EVs are happier living in the mid-to-high range unless you need the miles tomorrow.

## Public charging without the stress

1. **Check the app before you arrive.** Plug types, offline stations, and “occupied” lies are real. Electrify America, ChargePoint, Tesla (where you have access), and your car’s built-in planner all disagree sometimes — cross-check.
2. **Know your connector.** CCS, NACS, and adapters are not interchangeable folklore. Carry what you need; do not assume the site has it.
3. **Etiquette.** Unplug when you are done. Do not ice the stall for an hour after you hit your target. If someone is waiting, move when you can.
4. **Payment.** Set up accounts ahead of time. Fumbling QR codes in the rain is a skill you only need once.

## Road-trip charging strategy

- Plan **legs to ~20–30% arrival**, then charge into the band your car likes for the next hop (often mid-pack, not always 100%).
- Prefer **clusters of stalls** over a single lonely pedestal. Redundancy beats optimism.
- Build in **food + restroom** at the stop. Fast charging is already a break — use it.
- Watch **temperature and hills**. Cold weather and mountain grades change both range and charge curve.

## Mental checklist

| Situation | Prefer |
| --- | --- |
| Nightly commute | Home Level 2 |
| Errands in town | Destination Level 2 if available |
| Weekend getaway | Mix of Level 2 + one DCFC if needed |
| Long interstate | DCFC along the corridor |

## Bottom line

Master **home Level 2**, treat **DC fast charging** as a trip tool, and verify stations before you commit. Once that rhythm clicks, “where do I charge?” stops being the main question — and the trip planning gets interesting again.
