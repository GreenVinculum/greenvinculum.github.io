This setup is **borderline glamping** — still a tent and leave-no-trace habits, but with comfort prioritized over ultralight bragging rights. It needs a shelter that stays up, a sleep system that keeps you warm, and a kitchen that does not turn into a racoon buffet. This is the checklist I use for weekend trips — Shenandoah-flavored, but portable.

## Shelter

- **Tent with a real rainfly** and stakes that match the ground (rock stakes ≠ soft soil stakes).
- **Footprint / groundsheet** cut smaller than the floor so rain does not pool underneath.
- **Guylines ready.** Wind finds the one night you “didn’t need them.”

Pitch before dark once. Practice at home if the tent is new.

## Tarps (do not skip these)

A tarp is cheap insurance. I pack **two roles** whenever space allows:

### 1. Ground tarp / footprint

- Protects the tent floor from rocks, roots, and abrasion.
- Keeps moisture from wicked-up soil from soaking the bathtub floor.
- **Cut or fold it smaller than the tent floor** so rain running off the fly does not collect on the tarp and sneak under the walls.

A bare floor on rough ground wears out faster than any other piece of the shelter system.

### 2. Overhead / dining tarp

- Gives you a dry place to cook, change, and stash packs when the sky opens up.
- Keeps gear and people out of the tent vestibule so the sleeping space stays cleaner and less humid.
- In shoulder seasons it is often the difference between a miserable wet camp and a usable one.

Rig it high enough to stand under, with a taut ridge line and enough stake-out that a gust does not turn it into a sail. Angle one edge lower on the windward side so rain sheds away from the kitchen.

**Bottom line on tarps:** the rainfly keeps the bed dry; the ground tarp keeps the floor alive; the overhead tarp keeps the trip livable when weather sits on you for a day.

## Sleep system

This is where the glamping tilt shows. I do not sleep on the ground if I can help it:

1. **Cot** — gets you off cold, uneven dirt and makes getting in/out of the tent less of a yoga move.
2. **Foam air mattress on the cot** — cushion plus a bit more insulation; the combo is what actually feels like a bed.
3. **Sleeping bag rated colder than you need.** I run bags rated around **30°F on nights that sit near 50°F**. That buffer means you sleep warm without inventing a quilt stack at 2 a.m. when the forecast was “optimistic.”
4. **Clothes** — dry base layer dedicated to sleep; never sleep in sweaty day clothes if you can help it.

Optional but high-value: a small pillow (or stuff sack + fleece) and earplugs for the 2 a.m. owl conference.

## Kitchen (organized, not chaotic)

Keep the menu simple, but treat the kitchen like a workstation:

- **Kitchen bin** — one tote for stove, fuel, pots, utensils, spices, trash bags, and cleaning kit. Open the lid and cook; close it and the site stops looking like a yard sale.
- **Kitchen table** — a real surface to stage food, hold the stove, and keep knives/plates out of the dirt. Organization beats “balanced on a cooler” every time.
- Stove + fuel you have tested
- Pot, spoon, mug
- Food that does not need a chef
- Trash bag that goes **out** with you
- Bear / animal plan appropriate to the area (canister, hang, or keep-in-car rules)

Cook away from the tent. Eat away from the tent. Store smellables away from the tent. Future-you will thank past-you.

## Water

- Know whether the site has potable water.
- If not: filter / treat capacity for drinking **and** cooking.
- Carry a dedicated dirty bottle and a clean bottle so you stop guessing.

## Weather and clothing

Pack for the forecast **plus** one colder, wetter version of it:

- Rain jacket (not “water resistant” hope)
- Warm midlayer
- Dry socks
- Sun protection for exposed ridges

Mountain weather flips. Cotton as a primary layer is a lore item, not a strategy.

## Camp craft habits

| Habit | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Arrive with daylight | Better site choice, safer pitch |
| Walk the site | Widowmakers, drainage, privacy |
| Hang a lantern zone | Stops the “where is everything” spiral |
| Morning reset | Dry gear, pack trash, leave the pad cleaner |

## Leave no trace (the short version)

- Stay on durable surfaces
- Pack out all trash (including microtrash)
- Dig catholes where required — or use the facilities
- Keep fires only where allowed, small, and fully dead
- Respect wildlife distance; you are in their kitchen

## Minimal packing list

**Shelter:** tent, stakes, guylines, footprint / ground tarp, overhead tarp  
**Sleep:** cot, foam air mattress, cold-rated bag, pillow  
**Kitchen:** kitchen bin, kitchen table, stove, fuel, pot, utensils, food, trash bag  
**Water:** bottles + treatment  
**Clothes:** rain + warm + sleep set  
**Extras:** headlamp, first aid, map/offline maps, knife, power bank

## Bottom line

Good tent camping — even the borderline-glamping kind — is boring in the best way: dry sleep on a cot, a warm bag with margin, a kitchen that stays in its bin, and a site that looks untouched when you leave. Nail shelter, tarps, sleep, and smellables, and the mountains do the rest.
