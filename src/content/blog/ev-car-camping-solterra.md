Car camping in an EV is fantastic when power and sleep line up — and slightly annoying when the software does not. The **2026 Subaru Solterra** is a strong camping partner mechanically (quiet, AWD calm, real buttons). It is weaker on dedicated **Camp Mode** / pet-mode UX, so this guide is about making overnight stays work anyway.

## What the Solterra is good at

- **Quiet nights.** No engine ticking over. Once you are set, the cabin is a solid sleep shell.
- **Familiar SUV layout.** Flat-ish cargo with seats down, upright seating, and gear that packs like any compact crossover.
- **Fast charging on the way.** Road-tripping to a trailhead is easier when a 20→90% DC stop is measured in minutes, not hours.

## What you have to work around

There is **no polished one-tap Camp Mode**. Leaving climate running while you sleep or walk the dog means learning a safe sequence for *your* software version, confirming the car will not “helpfully” power down, and watching state of charge. Treat climate-on overnight as a **budgeted kWh cost**, not a freebie.

Until Subaru ships a proper mode: test at home in the driveway first. Know how to leave HVAC on, how the screens lock (or do not), and what happens when you walk away with keys.

## Power strategy

1. **Arrive with a plan, not a prayer.** Roll into camp with enough buffer that overnight climate (if used) does not strand you in the morning.
2. **Prefer sites with shore power** when you can — even a basic pedestal beats draining the traction pack for a fan and phone.
3. **If dry camping:** run climate in short bursts, use bedding and layers, and keep a 12 V / USB plan for phones and lights that does not depend on HVAC.
4. **Morning top-up.** Know the nearest Level 2 or DCFC before you commit to a remote site.

## Packing the Solterra cabin

- **Sleep:** cargo-area pad or folded mattress; window covers / reflectix for privacy and temperature.
- **Kitchen:** small stove outside the car; keep food smell out of the cabin when bears (or raccoons) are a thing.
- **Power:** headlamps, power bank, and only the 12 V accessories you actually need.
- **Leave a clear exit.** Do not bury the hatch latch under a gear avalanche.

## Site choice for an EV

| Priority | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Level ground | Sleep quality + door clearance |
| Cell signal | Maps, charger status, weather |
| Charger within ~30–60 min | Morning recovery option |
| Shade / wind break | Cabin temperature without HVAC |

## Solterra-specific tips

- Use **physical climate controls** so you are not hunting menus half-asleep.
- Rehearse your **overnight power sequence** once in a driveway.
- For multi-day loops, alternate **dry nights** with **town / charger nights** instead of stretching one charge heroically.

## Bottom line

The Solterra camps well when you respect the battery budget and accept that “Camp Mode” is still a DIY ritual. Pair honest SOC planning with a tidy cargo sleep setup, and EV car camping becomes one of the quieter, calmer ways to overnight in the mountains — software quirks included.
